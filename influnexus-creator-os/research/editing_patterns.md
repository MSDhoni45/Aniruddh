# Editing Patterns — v0.1

Observation date: 2026-11-18.

## Limits

No competitor video was watched. There are **no measured values** here for cut rate, shot length, zoom frequency, caption font, words per minute or music use for any creator. This file contains only (a) cited third-party observations and (b) labelled hypotheses, followed by the experiments that will replace both with our own data.

Labels: **MEASURED** (seen in a cited source), **THIRD-PARTY** (someone else's analysis; often a vendor), **HYPOTHESIS** (ours).

| Watch time is one of Instagram's three top ranking signals (Jan 2025 statement). | MEASURED (press) | https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/ |
| MKBHD prefers short-form made natively for the platform over chopped long-form. | MEASURED (interview, via summary) | https://techcrunch.com/?p=2345613 |

Hypotheses
- P1. A 30–40s reel holds best with one idea, five beats, and a visual change every 2–4 seconds. The 2–4s figure is a guess to be measured, not a benchmark.
- P2. For Hinglish explanation, moderate pace with clean diction beats maximum speed, because viewers are parsing two languages. Untested (experiment 5).
- P3. Cutting all breaths and pauses raises the 3-second hold but can lower completion if it reads as frantic.

## 2. Captions

| Observation | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 80.2% of "viral" clips in a vendor sample used captions; 78.6% used caption animation. | THIRD-PARTY (vendor sells captions; correlational) | https://www.opus.pro/blog/video-editing-tips |
| 2019 Verizon Media / Publicis Media survey (5,616 US adults): respondents reported watching with sound off most of the time on mobile; half said captions matter because they watch muted. | THIRD-PARTY (secondary reports of a US survey; self-reported behaviour; old) | https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/ |
| Meta internal research: captions increased view time by about 12% on average. | THIRD-PARTY (platform's own claim, secondary report) | https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/studies-find-captions-improve-engagement |
| One marketer's two-month A/B: captioned videos had ~5% higher total view time and ~16% higher reach. A separate sponsored test found no notable watch-time difference. | THIRD-PARTY (small tests, mixed result) | https://kapwing.com/resources/subtitle-statistics , https://www.techradar.com/pro/why-you-need-to-add-subtitles-to-your-online-videos-and-its-not-just-because-69-percent-of-viewers-watch-on-mute |
| Safe zones: guides disagree. Reported blocked areas on a 1080×1920 frame: top 210–270 px, bottom 310–480 px, right side 84–120 px. One guide says Meta specifies the top as 14%. No official pixel spec was confirmed. | THIRD-PARTY (conflicting) | https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/ , https://reap.video/blog/short-form-video-safe-zones , https://www.houseofmarketers.com/guide-to-safe-zones-tiktok-facebook-instagram-stories |

Hypotheses
- C1. Captions help; the size of the effect is uncertain (somewhere between zero and ~12% view time in the sources). Always-on captions are a cheap default.
- C2. 2–5 words per caption group, max two lines, is readable at reel speed. Our starting rule, not a measured norm.
- C3. Hinglish captions in Latin script; technical terms in English. Devanagari not tested.
- C4. Conservative safe area until verified in-app: keep captions inside the central ~900×1400 px, at least 450 px above the bottom edge and 270 px below the top.
- C5. One highlight colour for the key word per group; more than one reduces legibility.

| Product/project showcase openers led a vendor's hook ranking. | THIRD-PARTY (vendor) | https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026 |
| Instagram downranks aggregators who repost others' content; watermarked reposts perform worse (reported). | THIRD-PARTY (Buffer / press restating Instagram) | https://buffer.com/library/instagram-reels-instructions-and-ideas-for-small-businesses , https://screenrant.com/instagram-reels-tab-tips-for-discovery-no-watermarks/ |

Hypotheses
- V1. Show the real thing (the tool's screen, the actual output) within the first 8 seconds; a screen recording we made ourselves beats stock footage.
- V2. Keep the face visible (picture-in-picture or split) during b-roll to keep the personal connection.
- V3. Every visual should answer "what is being said right now"; decorative b-roll is noise.
- V4. Own footage only. Reposted clips are both an ethics problem and, per the reports above, a distribution risk.

## 4. Sound

No source with usable evidence on music, sound effects or voice processing was found for any creator. Everything below is HYPOTHESIS.

- S1. Voice first: speech clearly louder than any bed; no music under the hook.
- S2. Sound effects only on a real visual event (a reveal, a number), a few per reel at most.
- S3. Original audio rather than trending sounds for news/explainer content.
- S4. Since some share of viewers watch muted (US survey above; Indian share unknown), the reel must make sense with sound off.

## 5. Controlled experiments

Rules for all: change **one** variable; hold topic class, posting time, length and thumbnail style constant; minimum 6 reels per arm (more is better); alternate arms across days; read results at a fixed age (72 hours) from Instagram Insights. Do not judge on raw views alone. With small samples treat a difference under ~15% as noise.

| # | Variable | Arm A | Arm B | Deciding metric | Secondary | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Language | Hinglish voice + Hinglish (Latin) captions | English voice + English captions | Average watch time as % of length | Sends per reach; follows per reach | Non-follower reach share |
| 2 | Visual format | Face-cam led (face on screen >60% of time) | Demo led (screen/product >60%, face in corner) | Completion rate | Saves per reach; sends per reach | 3-second hold rate |
| 3 | Hook form | Question hook | Fact/statement hook (same topic, same first frame) | 3-second hold rate | Average watch time | Comment sentiment (no "clickbait" complaints) |
| 4 | Length | 30s cut | 38s cut of the same script (extra proof beat) | Completion rate **and** absolute average watch seconds (need both) | Sends per reach | Drop-off point on the retention graph |
| 5 | Pacing | Fast (tight cuts, pauses removed) | Moderate (natural pauses kept) at same length | Average watch time as % of length | Rewatch/loop rate; comments per reach | Comprehension check: pinned-comment question answer rate |

Notes
- Exp. 1 can be run on the same script recorded twice; that is the cleanest pair.
- Exp. 4: a shorter video almost always has higher completion. The 38s cut wins only if absolute watch seconds rise and sends per reach do not fall.
- Exp. 5: fix the word count and change only the delivery and cut tightness; record the resulting words per minute for both arms so "fast" and "moderate" become numbers.
- Also log for every reel: hook category (see `hook_patterns.md`), caption style, first frame type. These become free observational data.

Further experiments to queue (HYPOTHESIS-driven): captions on vs off; Latin vs Devanagari captions; source citation on screen vs in caption only; behind-the-scenes insert vs none (Karen X. Cheng's claim); CTA at end vs none.
