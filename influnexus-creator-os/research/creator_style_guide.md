# Influnexus Creator Style v0.2 (Global English)

Date: 2026-11-19. Scope: English AI/technology short videos for a global English-speaking audience, 30–40 seconds, Instagram first, then LinkedIn, X and YouTube Shorts.

**Language change (19 Nov 2026):** the default is now 100% natural conversational English, recorded in the creator's own Indian accent. The Hinglish rules in v0.1 are retired.

**Status: v0.1, pending real video measurement.** No competitor video was watched while writing this (platforms were inaccessible). The style below is an original synthesis: a few rules lean on cited sources; most are starting hypotheses. Nothing here is copied from another creator's wording, graphics or format.

Each rule is tagged:
- **[E]** evidence-backed — a source is cited. Evidence is mostly third-party or correlational, so "backed" means "has some support", not "proven".
- **[H]** starting hypothesis — to be tested. See `editing_patterns.md` section 5 for the experiments.

Sources are abbreviated; full URLs are in the reference list at the end.

---

## 1. Voice

1. Natural, conversational English that a viewer in Lagos, London, Manila or Mumbai understands on first listen. **[H]**
2. Your own voice and your own Indian accent. No borrowed American or British intonation, no forced slang ("no cap", "lowkey", "y'all"). The validator rejects a list of these. **[H]**
3. Short sentences: one idea each, usually under 15 words. **[H]**
4. Plain words over jargon. If a technical term is essential, say it once and explain it in the next sentence ("system card: the safety report that ships with a model"). **[H]**
5. Say the name and the number: "OpenAI released two models" rather than "something big happened". **[H]**
6. No corporate language ("leverage", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "revolutionise") and no fake excitement ("insane", "mind-blowing", "you won't believe"). The validator enforces a banned list. **[H]**
7. Give a take, not just the news: what happened, then what it means, labelled as your reading. **[E]** (news plus a personal take is a stated daily practice of Varun Mayya [S2]; the wording and opinions must be ours.)
8. Moderate pace with clean diction; don't race. Target words per minute will be set from your own recordings. **[H]**

### Storytelling principles from the reference creators (general, not copied)

None of these creators' videos could be watched from this environment, so each principle is a **[H]** drawn from their public positioning and interviews, not from measured delivery. Never reuse their lines, formats, graphics or catchphrases.

- **Cleo Abram:** start from a question the viewer already has; optimism with honest limits; plan the visual before the words [S3, S6].
- **MKBHD:** clarity and restraint; say what it is, who it's for, and the honest verdict; native short form rather than chopped long-form [S9].
- **Bilawal Sidhu:** show the real thing working; let the demo carry the surprise.
- **Rowan Cheung:** speed and density; the single most important development, stated plainly, then why it matters.
- **Mrwhosetheboss:** curiosity-led comparisons; one surprising detail people didn't know.

## 2. Personality

1. Curious, calm, a little sceptical. The friend who actually read the release notes. **[H]**
2. Optimistic about what people can build, honest about limits. **[E]** (optimistic framing is central to Cleo Abram's show positioning [S3]; that it suits our audience is **[H]**.)
3. Says "we don't know yet" or "that isn't confirmed" when that is the truth. **[H]**
4. Shows the work: our own screen, our own test, our own result. **[E]** (showing the process with the result is what Karen X. Cheng credits for her growth [S4]; showcase openers led one vendor's hook data [S5].)
5. No fear-selling ("your job is gone") without a sourced, specific claim. **[H]**

## 3. Script structure (30–40s)

| Time | Beat | Rule | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | **Hook** | One sentence, 6–10 words, contains the specific fact or the visible result. No greeting, no channel name. First frame already shows the subject (face plus on-screen text, or the demo itself). | **[H]** for the word count and timing. **[E]** for opening on the demo/result [S5, S4]. |
| 2–8s | **Context** | What it is and who made it, in two sentences. Source named on screen here. | **[H]** |
| 8–20s | **Proof** | Show it: our screen recording, the real output, the actual number. One demonstration, not three. | **[E]** that visuals should lead the script (Cleo Abram plans visuals first [S6]). Beat length is **[H]**. |
| 20–30s | **So what** | What it changes for the viewer. One concrete consequence. Our take, labelled as opinion. | **[E]** for including a take [S2]. Placement is **[H]**. |
| 30–40s | **Close** | One line that lands the point or loops back to the hook. One light ask at most (send to someone who needs it). End on the last useful word; no outro. | **[E]** that sends matter for reach to non-followers [S7]. Wording and position are **[H]**. |

Further script rules
1. One idea per reel. If it needs two, make two reels. **[E]** (compression as a discipline: Fireship's "30 minutes into 100 seconds" [S8]; one-idea rule itself is **[H]**.)
2. Write three columns: what is said, what is seen, where the claim comes from. **[E]** (three-column script reported for Cleo Abram [S6]. Adopted as a method, not a look.)
3. Make it for Reels from the start; do not chop long-form. **[E]** (MKBHD's stated preference [S9].)
4. Delete-test the hook: if removing the first sentence loses no information, rewrite it. **[H]**
5. The payoff must match the hook. No bait. **[E]** (clickbait that fails to deliver hurts retention, per third-party guidance [S10].)
6. Default length 30–38s. **[H]** (vendor data suggests 30–45s [S5]; Experiment 4 decides.)

## 4. Caption style

1. Captions always on. **[E]** (most "viral" clips in a vendor sample used them [S5]; a US survey reports heavy sound-off viewing [S11]; Meta reports ~12% more view time [S12]. Effect size is uncertain; one small test found none.)
2. 2–5 words per group. **[H]**
3. Maximum two lines on screen at once. **[H]**
4. English captions that match the spoken words exactly; numbers as digits; product names spelled as the company spells them. **[H]**
5. Safe margins: until verified in the app, keep captions inside the central ~900×1400 px of a 1080×1920 frame, at least 450 px from the bottom, 270 px from the top, clear of the right-hand button column. **[E]** (third-party guides; they disagree, so we take the most conservative values [S13].)
6. One highlight colour, used on the key word only. **[H]**
7. Captions match the spoken words exactly. No paraphrase, no added claims. **[H]**
8. Simple entrance animation at most; nothing bouncing. **[H]** (animated captions are common in a vendor's high performers [S5]; we choose restraint and will test.)
9. High-contrast text with an outline or soft backing so it reads on any background. **[H]**

## 5. Visual rules

1. Clean frame: uncluttered background, one subject. **[H]**
2. High contrast between subject, background and text. **[H]**
3. Subtle motion only: slow push-ins, gentle cuts. No shake, no flash transitions. **[H]**
4. Natural skin tones. No heavy filters, no beauty smoothing, grade for accuracy. **[H]**
5. Face stays visible. During screen demos, keep the face in a corner or split frame. **[H]** (Experiment 2 measures face-led vs demo-led.)
6. Change the visual when the idea changes, not on a timer. **[H]**
7. Use our own footage and our own screen recordings. **[E]** (reposted and watermarked content is reported to be downranked [S14]; also an ethics rule, section 7.)
8. 1080×1920, 9:16, full screen. **[E]** [S13]
9. Every b-roll shot must illustrate the sentence being spoken. **[H]**

## 6. Sound rules

1. Voice first. Speech is the loudest element in every second of the reel. **[H]**
2. No music under the hook. If there is a bed later, it sits well below the voice. **[H]**
3. Sound effects only on real visual events; a few per reel at most. **[H]**
4. Original audio. **[H]**
5. The reel must be fully understandable muted. **[E]** (sound-off viewing reported in a US survey [S11]; Indian share unknown.)
6. Record clean: close microphone, quiet room; fix audio before fixing colour. **[H]**

No source with evidence on music or sound design was found. This whole section is hypothesis except rule 5.

## 7. Ethics

1. **No copying.** Never reuse another creator's catchphrases, signature lines, graphics, templates, thumbnails or footage. Learning a method (visual-first scripting) is fine; imitating a recognisable look or line is not. **[H]** (our policy.)
2. **No reposted clips** presented as ours. If third-party footage is essential (a product launch clip), use only what is licensed or clearly permitted, keep it short, and credit it on screen. **[E]** [S14] plus policy.
3. **Attribute claims on screen.** Every factual claim shows its source (company blog, paper, official post) as on-screen text in the 2–8s beat, and again in the caption. **[H]** (our policy; modelled on the "sources" column in [S6].)
4. **Separate fact from opinion.** Say "meri rai mein" before a take. **[H]**
5. **Numbers need a date and a source.** No "studies show". **[H]**
6. **Disclose AI.** If a voice, avatar or image is AI-generated, say so on screen. **[E]** (disclosure is recommended in coverage of AI-clone short-form [S15].)
7. **Disclose sponsorships** with the platform's paid-partnership label. **[H]** (policy; legal requirements should be checked separately.)
8. **Correct publicly.** If a reel is wrong, pin a correction and fix or remove it. **[H]**
9. **No fear, no fake urgency, no invented controversy.** A "controversy" hook needs two real, named sides. **[H]**
10. **Do not benchmark by deception.** Research uses public information only. **[H]**

## 8. How we will know (metrics)

Track per reel from Instagram Insights: 3-second hold rate, average watch time (seconds and % of length), completion rate, likes per reach, sends per reach, saves per reach, follows per reach, non-follower share. **[E]** that watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach are the stated top signals [S7]. No external benchmark values are set in v0.1, because none could be measured.
