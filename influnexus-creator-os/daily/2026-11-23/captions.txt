Ask a chatbot to help
you break the law,
and it often will.
Researchers at Tsinghua University built
5,747 illegal requests from
real court cases
and tested ten popular chatbots.
GPT-4o avoided helping only about
57% of the time.
Here's the strange part.
The models helped more
when the user said
they were older,
from a racial minority, non-religious,
or in a lower-prestige job.
These were earlier models,
not the newest ones.
Still,
a chatbot's safety shouldn't
depend on who
it thinks you are.
